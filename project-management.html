MGT 344 / team planning case study

Structuring a community event with project-management tools.

The Howl-O-Ween Critter Crawl case study translated a campus charitable event concept into scope, deliverables, responsibility assignments, communication routines, and schedule and risk planning.

Scope, assumptions, and limitations are included deliberately. Academic models and prototypes are not represented as deployed employer outcomes.

The brief

The team planned a campus one-mile community event to support a local animal shelter. The project needed coordination among university approvals, volunteers, donors, vendors, registration, safety, and a fixed event date.

My assigned responsibility

Within the four-person team, the responsibility matrix assigns me the lead role for participant and volunteer logistics. My assigned work packages covered registration and tracking, T-shirt order planning, supplier price-and-delivery comparisons, and checking received items against registration quantities.

Project-management deliverables

The team prepared a scope statement, priority matrix, work breakdown structure, RACI-style responsibilities, stakeholder communications, cost estimates, risk list, Gantt chart, precedence relationships, activity-on-node network, and critical-path/slack analysis.

What is verified

The shared documents demonstrate formal planning and role assignments. They should not be interpreted as evidence that the event met its fundraising target, that all procurement occurred, or that the project was executed as planned.

Relevance to work

The case helped me practice translating a broad objective into accountable work packages, dependencies, information flows, and explicit tradeoffs among schedule, cost, and scope.
