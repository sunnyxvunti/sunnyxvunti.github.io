AnyLogic / discrete-event simulation

Testing the dining bottleneck before changing service capacity.

A service line can look busy without revealing which resource is actually constraining throughput. I used observed service-flow data and discrete-event simulation to explore options before proposing changes.

The question

When demand peaks, does the delay come from staffing, station design, arrival variability, or how flows interact downstream? A simulation makes these competing explanations testable.

Model scope

The project drew on more than 120 observed peak-lunch transactions in a Mercer dining setting. The model represented 11 stations and 16 staff, focusing on arrival and service behavior, queues, and the interactions between stations.

Analysis

Using AnyLogic discrete-event simulation, I explored bottlenecks and compared staffing and layout interventions under modeled demand conditions. The most favorable reported scenarios projected about an 18% reduction in peak wait time and about a 12% improvement in throughput.

Important limitation

These are model projections, not measured results from a deployed intervention. Simulation quality depends on calibration, the representativeness of the observed sample, and the accuracy of routing and resource assumptions.

Transferability

The method is useful for dining, healthcare, warehousing, field service, and other systems where arrivals vary and fixed capacity must respond to peaks.
