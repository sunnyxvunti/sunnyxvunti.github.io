Aramark / operational data quality

Turning inconsistent product records into actionable exception reports.

At a multi-location dining operation, a single source of truth is only useful when menu templates, inventory records, and product attributes agree. This work focused on locating mismatches and making them reviewable.

The operational question

How can an operator trust a report when product names, allergen labels, item mappings, and what is available in Transact do not always align? The first step is to make disagreement visible, not conceal it behind a single total.

My contribution

At Mercer dining, I supported Transact product-data maintenance and built Excel trackers for item descriptions, allergen and nutrition completeness, and review status across campus dining locations. For an Atlanta mobile-order reconciliation, I compared template entries with Transact records, identified unmatched items on both sides, and created a gap report organized for management review.

Methods and approach

The workflow separated matched items, template items without an identified Transact match, and Transact records not represented in the template. It also preserved review notes and relevant attributes so that a manager could investigate whether a difference came from naming, configuration, scope, or a genuinely missing item. Separately, I audited over 500 SKUs across four Mercer dining locations and used root-cause tools in operational improvement work.

What the evidence supports

The results are documented exceptions and a more structured reconciliation workflow—not proof that every discrepancy was resolved or that procurement savings were realized. Screenshots of internal systems and source workbooks are intentionally excluded from this public portfolio.

Why it matters

Data quality in operations is not a cosmetic concern. It shapes inventory visibility, customer-facing descriptions, product governance, and the quality of downstream decisions.
