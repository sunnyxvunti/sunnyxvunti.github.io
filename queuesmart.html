QueueSmart / AI-assisted operations prototype

A queueing model built to explore better staffing decisions.

QueueSmart is an exploratory decision-support application that uses queueing concepts and forecasting approaches to examine staffing and capacity tradeoffs under variable demand.

Scope, assumptions, and limitations are included deliberately. Academic models and prototypes are not represented as deployed employer outcomes.

The operational problem

When a service system gets overwhelmed, adding a person is sometimes the right decision—but not always at the right station or time. The useful question is how much capacity each scenario changes relative to demand and cost.

What I prototyped

Using AI-assisted programming tools, I developed a queue-analysis and scenario-comparison application featuring service rates, arrival patterns, station utilization, waiting-time estimates, and staffing/cost comparisons. The archive also includes forecasting and simulation components.

Engineering ideas

The work draws on M/M/s queueing theory, demand scenario testing, discrete-event simulation, and time-series forecasting. The prototype explores how decisions change when arrival demand shifts rather than treating average demand as sufficient.

Technical limitations

This project has not been established as a production deployment or a live IoT-integrated dining system. Some experimental forecasting components need additional validation; prototype outputs are not operational outcomes.

What I learned

A useful tool should let managers inspect assumptions, see the consequence of a scenario, and understand where the model stops being reliable.
